# Road B, completed: the FPGA popcount accumulator and the dataflow it re-opened (2026-07)

Road B is ADR-005's second answer to the readout wall: instead of adding
numbers **inside DRAM** (Road A — the in-DRAM carry-save adder tree, the
MVDRAM-faithful arm), aggregate them **in the FPGA readout path** — every
row read in DIFF-accum mode returns one 32-bit `popcount(row XOR ref)`
total (96 B on the wire) instead of 8 KB of row data. The two roads are
never blended in any headline (ADR-005); everything on this page is the
**FPGA-accelerated, rig-specific arm**, not part of the DRAM-PIM
reproduction.

## 1. Getting the datapath to actually work (builds 4 → 6)

Three silicon-found bugs, each with a Verilator reproduction in `rtl/`:

| build | fix | symptom it killed |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | `pop_count4` nibble-0xE case | undercounted totals |
| 5 | FWFT trailer framing | mis-framed drain messages under FIFO fill latency |
| 6 | **buffer_space conservation** | the deterministic "8 programs, then h2c errno-512 wedge" |

The build-6 story is the instructive one: DIFF-mode read beats are
consumed by the popcount path and never reach c2h, but the c2h budget
(`buffer_space`) only credited actual c2h transfers — a leak of
2·reads−2 units per program. 2048-unit budget ⇒ starvation at program 9,
`need_flush` looping forever, fetch stall, h2c failure. Cols-independent
(rdRow announces the full row), reproducible from clean state, and
present in **stock DRAM-Bender's streaming DIFF mode** as well
(upstream-relevant; patch offered alongside
`api-patches/0001` finalize-once).

Silicon verdict on the shipped build-6 image: 9/9 accum-suite cases
exact incl. toggle-back READ sanity; 60/60-program endurance at the
exact configuration that died at 9 (`docs/data/roadb/`).

## 2. What the accumulator is NOT good for (measured honestly)

The natural guess — "make the existing GeMV tree faster by reading
totals" — is wrong: every carry-save-tree readback carries row VALUES
the host must transport to the next tree level. A total cannot replace a
value. And at one read per program, program dispatch + receive-window
mechanics (~0.1–3 ms) dwarf the 8 KB-vs-96 B delta. The accumulator's
per-op wall win only appears when many **same-output** reads share a
program (totals sum), or at very large ops (see §4).

## 3. What it re-opened instead: the per-output product dataflow

With totals nearly free, the optimal Lane-2 dataflow inverts to the
shape MVDRAM itself describes (§V — products per output row, processor
aggregates popcounts):

    y[m] = Σ_i Σ_c FAC(i,qb)·FAC(c,rb) · popcount( W_i[m] AND x_c )

One fused program per product on the fastpath MAJ3 tuple: clone the
resident activation row, per-column-write the W row, MAJ3-AND against a
zero row, read the product row. The June design had priced this shape
out because per-product reads hit the exp0 readout wall; the July
measured cost model (dispatch-dominated, reads cheap) plus the
accumulator flipped it back. Implementation: `lane2/lane2_gemv_server.cpp`,
`LANE2_ACCUM=1` (READ-mode readout + host popcount) / `=2` (DIFF-accum
totals) — **identical program bytes in both arms**, so arm agreement
isolates the readout mechanism.

Results (bender 2, s86 subarray, build-6; full 16-cell table in
`docs/data/roadb/ab_full_table.md`):

| shape | qb | products | read arm | accum arm | tree arm (07-20 table) | read vs tree |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4096×4096 | 1 | 4 096 | **0.74 s** | 2.37 s | 8.61 s | 11.6× |
| 4096×4096 | 4 | 16 384 | **2.99 s** | 5.48 s | 34.09 s | 11.4× |
| 4096×11008 | 4 | 44 032 | **8.07 s** | 9.08 s | 34.35 s | 4.3× |
| 11008×4096 | 4 | 16 384 | **3.32 s** | 5.60 s | 91.53 s | **27.6×** |
| 32000×4096 | 2 | 64 000 | 11.72 s | 11.77 s | 17.04 s | 1.5× |
| 32000×4096 | 3 | 96 000 | 17.61 s | **16.72 s** | 25.65 s | 1.5× |
| 32000×4096 | 4 | 128 000 | 23.51 s | **20.80 s** | 33.97 s | 1.4× |

- **The product dataflow is 1.4–28× faster than the tree arm**, with
  exactly the predicted scaling: it is flat in K (the tree scales with
  K·qb·density), so the win is largest where the reduction is deep and
  the output narrow (11008×4096: 27.6×) and smallest at M=32000.
  Sparsity-independent throughout.
- **The accumulator's first outright wall wins** land precisely past
  the predicted ~50 K-product crossover: 32000×4096 qb3 (1.05×) and
  qb4 (**1.13×**, 20.80 vs 23.51 s).
- Exactness: numpy-EXACT at ≤512×256 volume in BOTH arms; at 4096²+
  volume the unvoted per-gate flake floor emerges (max count error 6–28
  on |y|≈2000, ≤0.3 % relative, per-run stochastic, identical character
  in both arms — compute noise, not readout).
- **Accum-vs-read**: marginal accum cost 128–145 µs/product beats the
  read arm's ~180 µs, but ~2.6 s of fixed DIFF entry/exit hygiene puts
  the wall crossover at ~50 K products/op — the 32000×4096 head shape
  runs at parity. The 128× wire collapse (8 KB → 96 B/product) is
  unconditional.
- **Stream integrity at scale**: every accum program carries ≥1 read ⇒
  one chunk per program in FIFO order; known-total sentinel x-loads
  every 64 products; kickers-until-parity at end-of-stream. 65 000
  programs → 65 000 totals in order, **zero** sentinel mismatches.

## 3b. The other FPGA lever, and why it waits: seq_engine

Road B is the **readback** accelerator. Its sibling — `rtl/seq_engine.v`,
Verilator-validated, integration staged — is the **command-issue**
accelerator (4 DDR commands per fabric cycle, fetch/decode/execute
bypassed; 8× on read bursts, 10.7× MAJ3 issue util). The per-program
server profile says why Road B comes first and seq_engine second:

    total 6.0 ms = wcol 1.3 + exec 1.0 + recv 3.1 + pop 0.2 + other 0.3

`recv` (readback) is the largest term and Road B's target; `exec`
(command issue) is what seq_engine removes. Flashing a command-issue
accelerator before the readback collapse lands in the production server
optimizes the second-largest term first — so seq_engine ships as a
validated module with a staged integration design (`rtl/SEQ_ENGINE.md`)
and waits for Road-B-into-production to make command issue the binding
constraint.

## 4. Where this goes next

- **W-residency clone loads**: keep weight rows resident as screened
  value rows and clone-load products (drops the 4.4 K-instruction
  per-column write from the loop) — amortized across iterations exactly
  as MVDRAM's own convention assumes (their weights are DRAM-resident).
- **Tiled-K single-output packing**: several K-tiles of ONE output per
  program — their totals sum correctly; the first shape where the
  accumulator wall-wins per op outright.
- **BitNet/Bonsai vertical layouts** put 2048 outputs on one row —
  whole-row totals are structurally blind there; a Lane-1 Road-B number
  requires an explicit layout-adoption decision (documented, not
  planned).

Full lab records: `roadb_build5/6` and `roadb_lane2` session dirs in the
workspace; condensed data in `docs/data/roadb/`.

## 5. Addendum (2026-07-21 late): W-residency clone-load products (LANE2_WRES)

Removing operand streaming from the product loop: LOAD pcwrites W
bitplane rows once into ~47 screened value rows; GEMV computes resident
products with a ~700-instruction clone-gate (both operands by RowClone)
instead of the ~4.4 K-instruction per-column-write gate. Mixed-run
telemetry: **clone gates 57–65 µs vs pcwrite gates 140–182 µs — ~2.7×
per product**; at full residency the product-arm floor projects to
~0.48 s for 4096×4096 qb2. This is MVDRAM's own resident-weights
convention made real on the reproduction rig. Honest trade: a few
≤2-count output deviations appear at small scale (clone-transfer flakes
plus W-row retention — resident rows are written once and, with
auto-refresh off in accum sessions, never rewritten); fix candidates
are a W-rewrite cadence (pays at iteration counts > 1) or aref-on.
Residency capacity is 0.56 % of a 4096-output slice per subarray — the
scale axis is more subarrays/banks, i.e. exactly the
characterization-transfer path in `UTILIZATION.md`'s bank addendum.
Data: `docs/data/roadb/ab_wres.md`.

## 6. Addendum (2026-07-21 PM): plane-packed multi-read totals (LANE2_PLANE_PACK)

The multi-read accum regime — several `rdRow`s inside ONE program, the
accumulator summing across all of them — had never been exercised. This
lever packs all qb plane-gates of one (m,c) product into a single
program, reads plane *i*'s product row 2^i times, and stores the
negative top plane complemented, so the program's one 32-bit total is
already the plane-weighted partial and the host just subtracts
2^(qb−1)·pc(x) (in-extent identity pc(~W∧x) = pc(x) − pc(W∧x)).

**It works, exactly**: with all planes resident, the packed arm is
numpy-exact and byte-identical across runs (128×32 qb2); replicated
back-to-back reads are electrically clean (`rdRow` opens with its own
PRE→ACT). Per-plane-gate 32–53 µs vs 59–65 for the per-plane resident
arm at moderate M; at M=11 the recv cadence dominates (71–73 µs) —
wall-neutral until residency capacity grows (`ROADMAP.md`).

**The incident worth reading** (why this addendum exists): the first
bring-up produced deterministic, sentinel-clean, wrong tail outputs.
Chain: an app-side IMEM guard with a bytes-vs-instructions unit bug
(allowed 16000 insts against the 8192-inst IMEM) let over-size spilled
programs through → the platform's own IMEM gate refused them (skip, no
send) → the accum stream shortened → end-of-stream kicker programs
backfilled the missing totals with pc(x)-valued chunks → every skipped
output computed exactly y = −pc(x), deterministically, with all
integrity sentinels green. Diagnosis closed by predicting the corrupted
values from the mechanism (−24/−31/−42 … all matched). Fixes: gate in
instructions, and `oversize_skips()` observability
(`api-patches/0004`) — the accum GEMV now refuses results if any
program in the batch was refused. If you build accum-total systems on
this stack: **a result stream that maps 1:1 onto executed programs must
verify the programs actually executed.**

## 7. Build 7: SEG_POP in production (2026-07-21, post-flash)

`rtl/README.md` (build 7) has the mode itself: per-32-bit-segment
popcount readout, 2048 B/row, READ/DIFF untouched. Production wiring
(`app/test_bitnet_server.cpp` `PIM_SEGPOP=1`): matvec row reads run in
SEG_POP (the received bytes ARE the per-segment popcounts, host
`segment_popcount` deleted), while LOAD-verify and decay-detect paths
transparently drop to READ for raw bytes. Silicon validation
`app/test_segpop_hw.cpp`: 3 pattern cases 2048/2048 exact + toggle-back
+ build-6 suite 9/9 on the same image.

Full-model A/B (bonsai_1bit fused V2S single-track, 8 tok, bender 2):
**token-identical** output with `PIM_SEGPOP=1`; SEG_POP-arm per-program
profile total ~3.0 ms (recv 1.5–1.8 ms, host pop 0.0) — and the 8-token
wall **neutral** (267.1 → 264.7 s). We then instrumented instead of
guessing (`req-prof`, per-request gap/body/handler in the dispatcher)
and the decomposition kills two hypotheses at once:

- gap 0.15–0.19 ms, body ≈ 0 — the client keeps the pipe full; there
  is no hidden client/transport half (our first guess — wrong).
- handler 3.0–3.1 ms ≈ exactly one program round-trip, ~5,400
  requests/forward × 3.2 ms ≈ the measured 17.6 s/forward. The wall
  IS programs — but `recv` is **XDMA-latency-dominated, not
  byte-dominated**: cutting 8 KB → 2 KB removes bytes, not the
  ~1.5 ms fixed per-read round-trip. That is the whole story of the
  wall-neutral result.
- The obvious amortizer, `PIM_INLINE_BITPLANES=4` (batch 4 chunks per
  program), measured a **net loss** (171.6 vs 150.0 s / 2 tok): each
  V2S request already carries ~1 program, so K-batching inside a
  request adds instruction volume without removing round-trips.

The remaining lever is therefore **fewer requests**: the client splits
each projection into ~28 per-chunk requests; the V2S wire format
already supports many units per request. Client-side chunk batching
(fewer, larger requests → fewer, larger recv windows) is the top
open item (`ROADMAP.md` §B.3b).

BitNet ternary (2.4B, dual-track, single-DIMM fused, 8 tok): the same
verdict at ~2× the programs/token — **"Paris", token-identical**, wall
1077.6 → 1060.0 s (1.6 %). SEG_POP is therefore correctness-clean on
BOTH production model families and wall-neutral on both, for the same
latency-dominated-recv reason. The ~25.6 s/tok projection assumed the
5.9 ms *byte-dominated* profile of the older non-fused config and is
superseded. The readback byte collapse is banked (it becomes decisive
exactly when transfers get bigger — i.e., under the request-batching
lever above); `req-prof` ships in the server so the decomposition is
reproducible.
