1 · The bug that was a door

In May 2026 our in-DRAM BitNet weight pool started corrupting itself. The loader used RowClone — a timing-violating ACT(src) …t12… PRE …t23… ACT(dst) double-activation that copies a row inside a subarray — and every clone from row 38640 to row 38424 also wrote the source data into row 38648 = 38640 ⊕ 8, a row no command ever named. The pool stride was 8, so every load silently overwrote the row one stride above its source. The fix was trivial (stride 16). The observation was not: the deposit was deterministic, byte-exact for all 8 KB, every trial.

Everything below came from pulling on that thread with constellation probes: write distinct fingerprints into ±{1,2,4,8,…,512} XOR/arithmetic neighbors of a row, fire one primitive, read everything back, classify each row as untouched / received-source / flaked. One probe run takes minutes; it turned out to be a microscope for the chip's address plumbing.

2 · Whose mechanism it is — and what was actually new

Attribution, settled 2026-07: the underlying effect is the multi-row co-activation lattice of SAFARI's SiMRA work (simultaneous many-row activation), the same family PULSAR builds its Multi-RowInit primitive on — the SiMRA authors confirmed the match on their tracker, verified against our data. The mechanism is theirs. What follows is what our measurements add on top of that mechanism.

The published framing of many-row activation is statistical: PULSAR characterizes primitives by per-bitline success rates (e.g. 97.91% MAJ3 best-case with input replication, "up to 32 rows"). Our central finding is that on our parts the same physics behaves as an exact, closed-form, transferable law:

  • Byte-exact: a firing member receives all 2048 × 32-bit segments of the source, 2048/2048, every trial.
  • Zero-exception member selection: a predecoder rule predicts which candidate rows fire — 1,691/1,691 member-observations, no exceptions, timing-invariant.
  • Transferable: the deposit structure is byte-identical across all 16 banks of a die and across our two SK hynix modules — which are two different part numbers, one dual-rank (a 3,941-fault set matched byte-for-byte, surviving even a rig change).

That gap — success-rate phenomenon vs exact law — is what turns a reliability hazard into an addressing primitive.

3 · The selection law

Fire ACT(A) → PRE → ACT(B) inside one address block, with d = A ⊕ B the differing bits. The candidate set is the coset {A ⊕ S : S ⊆ bits(d)}. Which members actually receive the data follows the row decoder's predecoder group structure — address bits are consumed in pairs, and a member fires if and only if its flip-set respects every pair:

A ⊕ S fires  ⟺  ∀ groups g ∈ {(1,2),(3,4),(5,6),(7,8)} :  S ∩ g ∈ { ∅ , d ∩ g }
1,691/1,691 observations, zero exceptions · timing-invariant · byte-identical on both our SK hynix modules (two different part numbers) · hynix production dies only — our two Micron DIMMs do not follow it (chip-specific decoders)

Intuition: within each predecoder pair you either leave the pair alone or flip it exactly the way the A→B transition flips it — partial flips of a pair don't correspond to a reachable decoder glitch state. Single-bit members (A ⊕ 2^b for b ∈ bits(d)) fire on every module we own, which is why the first symptom anyone sees is the "+8 shadow row".

Fig. 1 — the coset and the rule. One double-activation from A toward B = A⊕17 deposits A's data into the law-selected members of {A⊕S : S ⊆ {bit0, bit4}} — here both single-bit members and the destination — while law-rejected rows and non-members stay untouched, byte-for-byte. Recipe: test_spread.cpp, 25-offset constellation × 14 primitive cases.

4 · The geometry: two different structural units

DRAM datasheets give you rows, banks, and not much in between. The probes resolve two distinct internal structures that matter for any in-DRAM computation — and they are not the same size on our parts:

structuresize (our hynix production dies)what it scopeshow we measured it
sense-amplifier segment ("subarray")640 rowsRowClone / charge-sharing copy reach; MAJ tuple localitycopy success vs row distance; subarray-transfer probe
predecoder block~512 / 1024 rows (bit-pair scoped)the co-activation lattice: which rows can co-fire at all; the selection law's scopeselection-law sweep + boundary atlas (§5)

The crucial cross-check: deposits cross sense-amp segment boundaries while staying inside a predecoder block — the law is decoder wiring, not bitline physics. Conversely a tuple shifted into the next segment keeps its short-range behavior but changes its long-range couplings (§5). Any allocator for in-DRAM compute has to plan block-relative, not segment-relative — this single fact explains transfer results that once looked contradictory.

5 · The boundary atlas — the long-range member is block-anchored

Sweep the same calibrated tuple across a fine grid of row shifts and record which long-range member fires on a far-destination RowClone (2026-07-22, atlas_d*.log):

tuple shift+0+128+256+384+512+640+768+1024
long deposit (offset)+256+128(none)*−128−256nonenone†none
short deposit−1−1−1−1−1−1−1−1

The offsets look like a sign-flipping mystery until you notice they all point at one fixed absolute row (45597 for this tuple family): the tuple moves, the landing row doesn't. * At +256 the fired row is the landing row — the "(none)" is degenerate self-coincidence. † The +768 case is the decisive negative: the landing row sat at probed offset −512 and stayed clean — outside its block, the coupling is gone, not merely out of view.

Sharpest form (re-derived from raw logs, 2026-07-22): the firing long-range member is never the single-bit ⊕256 partner (it stays untouched at every shift — matching this die's ⊕-profile, which lacks 256) — it is whichever multi-bit coset member (⊕768 at +0, ⊕896 at +128, …) lands on the block-anchored row. Position picks the member; the landing row is the invariant. Single-bit XOR intuition misses this in both directions.
Fig. 2 — the boundary atlas. Shifted tuples (●) all deposit onto the same block-internal row: the deposit "offset" shrinks as the tuple approaches it and flips sign past it, because only the tuple moves. Outside the block the coupling vanishes — the +768 probe is decisive, since it had the landing row in-constellation at −512 and it stayed clean. The "moving offset" is an artifact of the moving tuple; the physical coupling targets a fixed block-internal row. This is why bank-to-bank transfer is free (same intra-block position) while subarray shifts change the long-range picture (§9).

6 · Conditionality, the null control, and self-pollution

Deposits are conditional, not ambient

With the fired row outside lattice conditions (cross-block destination, non-tuple center), the same primitives produce zero deposits on every bank — only scattered sub-threshold flakes (audit_bank*.log, null config). The spread is not "RowClone is leaky"; it is a precise co-activation with preconditions. The timing envelope on our hynix production parts: nothing at t12 ≤ 1, deposits across t12 = 5…30; t23 = 1 and 2 both deposit (our Micron DIMMs suppress some members at t23 = 2) — which is why a bitline-charge-only story doesn't cover the data.

MAJ3 pollutes its own neighborhood while it computes

The MAJ3-timing double-activation (t12=0, t23=0) deposits the first row's pattern into open rows at specific vulnerable offsets during the operation itself — we call it self-pollution. It explains an entire class of "tied" results on our lattice-poor DIMMs, and it is a correctness hazard for any compute-in-tuple mapping.

The closest published peer — and the direction still ours

The nearest published characterization is PuDGhost (Kubo et al., SAFARI / UTokyo, arXiv 2606.19119): on 96 SK hynix DDR4 chips they show that non-operand data biases SiMRA compute outputs — adjacent non-activated rows shift a result by ≤10% (monotonic, strictly adjacent), and concurrently-computing columns by ≤48% at 16-row activation (bitline coupling on opposite-parity, shared sense-amp side on same-parity) — with errors that are not column-independent, plus on-chip screening mitigations (CS-1 / CS-2). That is direct silicon corroboration of our disturbance axis: it explains our B1 5/5/5 balanced-mask rule (their inter-column class) and residents-amplify (their adjacent-row class), and its "errors are correlated across columns" result endorses our correlated-margin treatment. But PuDGhost runs the read direction — environment → output, non-operand data disturbing a computed result. Self-pollution above runs the write direction — the operation depositing into non-activated rows (op → environment). Those are complementary, not the same, and the deposit / write direction is still ours.

7 · The invariance ladder — what is wiring, what is lottery

Fig. 3 — what varies at which level. Across DIMM families everything is chip-specific — D0's pool stride 16, D2's ⊕-set {1,2,4,8,…,512} without 256, D1's ⊕2 signature — and only the method transfers. The 2026-07-22 completion run made the top rung airtight: all 16 banks constellation-probed, deposit-row sets md5-identical; the only inter-bank difference is a deterministic ~4-cell sub-threshold fringe class ({1–3, 8–11} vs the rest) on one non-production case — margin, not lattice.
  • Banks: bank 0's calibration, transferred verbatim to 15 never-calibrated banks → classification-identical probes and token-identical full-model inference. 350/350 constellation rows × 14 cases per bank.
  • Dies: a 3,941-fault XOR-spread set byte-identical across our two SK hynix modules — different part numbers, one dual-rank, surviving a full rig migration. The invariance follows the die design, not the module SKU; calibration files transfer wholesale.
  • Parts: each DIMM family has its own member patterns (and our two Micron DIMMs don't obey the hynix-die law at all) — the probes and the law-fitting method are what transfer, not the constants.

The boundary the ladder assumes: copy is exact, compute is per-process

Everything on the DESIGN rung is a claim about address-lattice copies — RowClone deposits, coset broadcasts, member selection. It does not extend to MAJ3 analog compute output. A raw per-operation MAJ3 popcount is not bit-exact across two server processes: on DIMM 2 the same op swung by 28/459/463/283 of 512 ones between separate bitnet-proj-server boots — a per-boot operating point (the upper-DQ-half margins move with the init MRS/ZQ/VREF class) — while staying deterministic within a process. What holds across processes is the correlation (0.997–0.9998); corruption is what collapses it. So the exactness gate for anything touching compute is per-op correlation on a same-process A/B (v2_oracle / mm3d-verify), never a cross-process per-op or token compare — those are invalid gates by construction. Copy and deposit sit on the DESIGN rung; MAJ3 compute output sits with the margins on LOTTERY.

The physics under that split showed up sharply in a 2026-07-28 experiment (X-master): a RowClone establishes its destination at charge-shared, not write-driven, cell levels, so the copied activation is a hair off — in-DRAM x′ ≠ x. Read back once, x′ matches byte-for-byte and every atlas result above holds. But reuse that same cloned row as a repeated compute operand and it never clears the exactness bar: the offset is a constant per-operation error rate, not an accumulating one — masters refill every round, so the seed-from-clone path sits at corr ≈0.94–0.97, 0/2048 bit-exact, depth-independent across 8–80 rounds at production density (while the write-driven production paths stayed bit-exact 2048/2048 at every depth to 80 rounds) and was closed negative. This is why the "free shadow copy" (§8) is real for storage and single reads, and why persistent-weight refresh (2.6×) stands by re-cloning a fresh operand each pass rather than letting one decay — a clone is a copy, not a substitute for a write-driven operand under repeated compute.

8 · Turning hazards into tools

Everything above started as a corruption bug. Mapped, it became an instrument set — each item below is a silicon-validated primitive. Whether a primitive earns a place in production is a separate, measured question: several of these do, and one flagship application built on the coset broadcast did not — the honest footnote is below the table.

Safe-loadchoose the clone pair-offset so every law-selected member lands on don't-care rows — RowClone into a live tuple, corruption-free by construction16/16 fast tuple loads, zero corruption (2026-07-17)

The deposit lever that didn't pay off — M3 coset-deposit (server PIM_BCAST_LOAD, resolved negative 2026-07-26, publicly retracted PR #16, merged 07-28; trailing pointer annotation in PR #18; server machinery excised, −286 lines). The idea was to use the coset broadcast to pre-deposit a shadow weight that the MAJ3 body would read instead of re-cloning. It doesn't: the body recomputes its row from the pool, so the shadow is never read — 99.4% of the "deposits" were d=0 no-ops, and the only live effect was write-elision (on ~87% stale content), a ceiling under 9% of the token wall. The lattice deposit primitive stays bit-exact, zero-leak, one-op fan-out — the physics in §§3–7 is untouched; only this production lever is dead.

9 · The practical payoff: calibrate once, run the die

The expensive artifact in this whole system is a calibrated MAJ-perfect tuple (a 30–40 h sweep per subarray). The atlas tells you exactly what that investment buys:

move acrosslatticeyou re-do
banks (same die)identicalmargin re-screen only — the loader's byte-verify is the screen (minutes)
dies (same model)identicalmargin re-screen only
subarrays (same bank)short-range transfers; long-range is block-position-dependentpool layout re-derivation (one spread probe) + margin screen
parts (different model)chip-specificfault sweep + law re-fit — the method, not the constants
Consequence: 16-bank scale-out on a characterized die is a config exercise — proven end-to-end when bank 0's calibration ran a full model token-identical on virgin banks on the first try. The die is ~99.99% idle during host-orchestrated compute; the lattice map is what makes claiming that idle silicon cheap.

11 · Open questions

  • Why that landing row? The block-anchor arithmetic (why 45597 for this family) isn't derived from first principles yet — we have the invariant, not the decoder netlist story.
  • Cross-manufacturer member rules: our Micron DIMMs break the hynix-die law — is there a different pair structure, or none?
  • Self-pollution scope: vulnerable-offset maps exist for our dies; is it universal to MAJ-timing double-ACTs on other parts?
  • Predecoder-block size: 512 vs 1024 behavior differs by bit pair on our parts — a finer atlas could pin the full pair→scope map.
